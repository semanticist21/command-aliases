# Korean editing patterns

This reference adapts Korean editing categories from DaleSeo's MIT-licensed `humanizer` skill v1.6.0. Original project authorship remains with DaleSeo. These are editorial prompts, not an authorship detector or confidence score.

- Punctuation: excessive commas, English-style placement, and decorative colons.
- Spacing: mechanically uniform dependent-noun and auxiliary-verb spacing that conflicts with the register.
- Part-of-speech balance: noun chains, nominalization, and too little verb/adjective variety.
- Vocabulary: generic importance words, avoidable abstraction, literal English phrasing, unnecessary `-들`, pronouns, and demonstratives.
- Structure: uniform sentence lengths, repeated three-part lists, excessive connectors, and mechanically consistent honorific endings.
- Translation-like constructions: repeated `에 대해`, `통해`, `있어서`, `가지고 있다`, `되어진다`, `에 의해`, `할 수 있다`, and `것이다`.

Load only categories relevant to the draft. Preserve meaning and register; natural variation is not a goal by itself.
